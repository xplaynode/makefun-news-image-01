# Tournament — news-image-01 local pairwise judging

Judged with the RULES rubric, in order: (1) specific events of 13 Nov 2026,
recognisable; (2) selection & breadth; (3) reads clearly from picture + short
labels; (4) faithful (claims labelled as claims); (5) craft — one clear
composition, legible at ~300px, no garbled text. All 12 candidates carry the
same seven stories from news-notes.md unless noted: Senate summons ex-Nvidia
CEO · Zelensky meets US envoys · drone hits Russian warship · Iran claims US
radar strike · FBI: N. Korea $400M crypto hack · Microsoft global outage ·
Kane breaks England record.

## First-round pairs

- **c01-grid beats c02-globe** — c02 repeats the Zelensky story in two panels
  (only 6 distinct stories) and hides pseudo-text inside its outage screen;
  c01 has all 7 stories once, letter-perfect captions, zero stray text.
- **c03-frontpage beats c04-darkposter** — both flawless on text; c03's scenes
  are specific (a real hearing room, flags, a ship under drones) where c04's
  are pictograms the labels must carry. c04 is the best pure-icon poster and
  the punchiest at 300px, but reads as a diagram, not a picture of the day.
- **c05-mappins beats c06-screenprint** — c05 covers all 7 stories with a
  geographic logic; c06 is gorgeous print craft but covers only 6 (no Kane)
  and its geodesic dome reads oddly for a radar. Both mid-field: c05's
  medallions are small and its map loose; c06 stays an honourable mention.
- **c07-photocollage beats c08-symbolic** — c08 is beautiful but undated and
  unlabelled, so it fails the top two criteria (a viewer cannot tell which
  day, or exactly what, it sums up). c07 is dated, labelled and realistic —
  but flawed: two big empty white boxes flank its Kane photo.
- **c09-comic beats c10-swiss** — c10's pictograms are too generic (blank
  flags, a chair and table, a monitor with a medical-looking cross); c09's
  panels are unmistakable and its yellow caption bars are the most legible
  text in the field.
- **c11-tvwall beats c12-watercolour** — both have all 7 stories with perfect
  captions; c11's photoreal screens are dramatic and specific, c12 is gentle
  and its warship is never clearly struck. c12's sad-face office windows are
  its best idea.

## 300px legibility test (semifinalists c01, c03, c04, c09, c11)

- c03 fades: thin serif headlines and a washed palette at 300px.
- c04 stays punchy but pictogram-flat.
- c11 reads well, though its FBI screen goes dark at 300px.
- c09's captions are the biggest in the field; its scenes stay dramatic.
- c01 stays fully legible — every caption readable, every scene identifiable.

## Semifinals and final

- **c01 beats c03** on the 300px test and colour clarity; c03's lead-story
  hierarchy is elegant but its six smaller headlines are the weakest text in
  the top group, and it carries faint pseudo body-text.
- **c11 beats c09** on faithfulness and finish: c09 shows the Bahrain radar
  dome exploding (the story is a *claimed* strike) and hides two misspelled
  micro-labels ("SYTEM ERROR") in its outage panel; c11 is photoreal, exact,
  and its "SYSTEM ERROR" screens are spelled right.
- **Final, c01 beats c11**: criteria 1–2 tie (same 7 stories, both dated).
  On 3 (reads), c01's flat scenes are literal and evenly lit — nothing hides
  in shadow at any size — while c11's darkest screen (the hacker) sinks at
  300px. On 5, both are clean; c01's single flat style is the more coherent
  composition. c01 is also the only finalist with literally no text anywhere
  except its seven perfect captions and header.

## Winner

**c01-grid.png** — "THE WORLD ON 13 NOV 2026": a flat editorial 7-tile grid.
Runner-up: c11-tvwall. Third: c09-comic.
